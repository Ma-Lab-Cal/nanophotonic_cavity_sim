# Project Objective Summary

## What this repo designs

This repository designs **suspended silicon-nitride (SiN, n ≈ 2.0) one-dimensional photonic-crystal nanobeam cavities resonant with the rubidium D2 line at 780 nm (384 THz)**, intended as a cavity-QED interface between a single tweezer-trapped neutral ⁸⁷Rb atom and a waveguide/fiber photonic channel. The code (`main_code/`, `0326_TAPEOUT/`) implements the full design pipeline:

1. **Band-structure engineering** (MPB in `bandstructure_mpb.py`, or Tidy3D) — find a *mirror* unit cell whose TE bandgap is centered on 384 THz, and a *defect* unit cell whose dielectric (or air) band edge crosses 384 THz.
2. **Cavity assembly** (`cavity.py`) — defect section whose geometry is interpolated cell-by-cell into the mirror geometry by a cubic polynomial (`defect.py`), flanked by Bragg mirrors and linear tapers into the feeding waveguide. Hole geometries include rectangles, ellipses, and intruded/extruded **sawtooth** cells (`crystal.py`), the latter giving strong field concentration in vacuum where an atom can sit.
3. **Waveguide impedance matching** — the final taper lattice constant is set to the Bragg condition of the waveguide standing wave, `a_last = λ₀ / (2 n_eff)`, with field nodes centered on the holes to minimize scattering (following Knall et al., PRL 129, 053603).
4. **FDTD verification and atom-physics post-processing** (`simulation.py`, Tidy3D cloud) — resonance frequency and total Q, **directional Q** (loss budget per simulation face, distinguishing the intentional waveguide port from scattering), mode volume, Purcell factor `(3/4π²)(Q/V)`, position-resolved coupling `g(r) = d√(ω_c / 2ħε₀ V(r))` using the Rb D2 dipole moment d ≈ 3.58×10⁻²⁹ C·m, cooperativity `C(r) = 4g(r)²/(κγ)`, and end-to-end photon collection efficiency `η(r) = (κ_out/κ_tot) · C/(C+1) · η_fiber`.
5. **GDS export** for fabrication (tapeout designs in `0326_TAPEOUT/`, designed at 150 nm and 250 nm film thickness — the 150 nm film shifts the first band edge from ~377 THz to ~410 THz, bracketing the 384 THz target).

## What makes a good cavity for neutral-atom qubit–waveguide coupling

The figure of merit is **not** raw Q. The objective is to maximize the probability that a single photon emitted by (or absorbed by) the trapped atom goes into one chosen waveguide mode, at a position where an atom can actually be trapped. Quantitatively:

**η = [C/(C+1)] · [κ_wg/κ_tot] · η_fiber,   with C = 4g²/(κγ)**

(γ/2π ≈ 6 MHz for Rb D2). This decomposes into four requirements:

1. **Large cooperativity at the atom location, C ≫ 1.** Since g ∝ 1/√V, this means small mode volume (state of the art V ≈ 0.4–0.9 (λ/n)³, g/2π ≈ 0.3–1 GHz at realistic atom positions) and *enough* total Q. Crucially, g must be evaluated ~200–300 nm from the dielectric surface — the closest standing-wave antinode of a tweezer retro-reflected off the beam — not at the field maximum inside the dielectric. Surface (Casimir–Polder) forces set a hard floor of z₀ ≈ 100–130 nm. C ≳ 10–35 gives atom–photon entanglement fidelity ≳ 0.9 (error ~ ln C / C); the best demonstrated single-atom values are C ≈ 27–71 (Lukin/Vuletić, Bernien groups). Air-mode / sawtooth geometries push field energy into vacuum to raise g(r) at the trap site.

2. **One-sided, overcoupled output: κ_wg/κ_tot → 1.** Losses must be dominated by the intentional waveguide port, not radiation/scattering. In practice: many mirror cells on the back side, few (3–5) on the coupling side, so Q_scattering ≫ Q_waveguide. This deliberately *sacrifices* total Q (and some C) for extraction efficiency — the optimum κ_wg exceeds the critical-coupling point. The repo's directional-Q analysis exists precisely to compute this budget. Measured outcoupling fractions of 10–40% were the dominant loss in the closest predecessor work (Menon thesis); >60% is the goal.

3. **The "bad-cavity" (Purcell) regime: κ ≳ g ≫ γ, with C ≫ 1.** Strong coupling is not required; a fast κ (GHz-scale, i.e., Q ~ 10⁴–10⁵ at 384 THz) gives broadband, fast photon extraction and protocol rates ultimately limited by 1/κ. Hence high *intrinsic* (scattering-limited) Q ~ 10⁵–10⁶ from gentle confinement, then loaded Q intentionally lowered through the coupling mirror.

4. **Compatibility with a trapped atom and with fabrication.** Beam thickness/width set the tweezer reflection phase and hence the trap distance z₀ (a sawtooth function of thickness — a genuine design trade against g and Q); the taper must be Bragg-matched to the waveguide to avoid scattering; lattice-constant-only tapers are the most fabrication-robust knob (e-beam hole *positions* are far more reproducible than hole *sizes*); the design should tolerate a few-nm dimensional error, with post-fabrication thermal tuning covering the residual.

The photonic recipe that delivers (1)–(3) is the "gentle confinement" / Gaussian-envelope method: taper the mirror strength (via filling fraction or lattice constant) quadratically from the defect center so the field envelope is Gaussian, leaving minimal Fourier content inside the light cone. That is exactly the architecture the `cavity`/`defect`/`mirror`/`taper` classes implement.
