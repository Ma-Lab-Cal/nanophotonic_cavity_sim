# Project Objective Summary

## What this repo designs

This repository designs **suspended silicon-nitride (SiN, n ≈ 2.0) one-dimensional photonic-crystal nanobeam cavities resonant with the rubidium D2 line at 780 nm (384 THz)**, intended as a cavity-QED interface between a single tweezer-trapped neutral ⁸⁷Rb atom and a waveguide/fiber photonic channel. The code (`main_code/`, `0326_TAPEOUT/`) implements the full design pipeline:

1. **Band-structure engineering** (MPB in `bandstructure_mpb.py`, or Tidy3D) — find a *mirror* unit cell whose TE bandgap is centered on 384 THz, and a *defect* unit cell whose dielectric (or air) band edge crosses 384 THz.
2. **Cavity assembly** (`cavity.py`) — defect section whose geometry is interpolated cell-by-cell into the mirror geometry by a cubic polynomial (`defect.py`), flanked by Bragg mirrors and linear tapers into the feeding waveguide. Hole geometries include rectangles, ellipses, and intruded/extruded **sawtooth** cells (`crystal.py`), the latter giving strong field concentration in vacuum where an atom can sit.
3. **Waveguide impedance matching** — the final taper lattice constant is set to the Bragg condition of the waveguide standing wave, `a_last = λ₀ / (2 n_eff)`, with field nodes centered on the holes to minimize scattering (following Knall et al., PRL 129, 053603).
4. **FDTD verification and atom-physics post-processing** (`simulation.py`, Tidy3D cloud) — resonance frequency and total Q, **directional Q** (loss budget per simulation face, distinguishing the intentional waveguide port from scattering), mode volume, Purcell factor `(3/4π²)(Q/V)`, position-resolved coupling `g(r) = d√(ω_c / 2ħε₀ V(r))` using the Rb D2 dipole moment d ≈ 3.58×10⁻²⁹ C·m, cooperativity `C(r) = 4g(r)²/(κγ)`, and end-to-end photon collection efficiency `η(r) = (κ_out/κ_tot) · C/(C+1) · η_fiber`.
5. **GDS export** for fabrication (tapeout designs in `0326_TAPEOUT/`, designed at 150 nm and 250 nm film thickness — the 150 nm film shifts the first band edge from ~377 THz to ~410 THz, bracketing the 384 THz target).

## What makes a good cavity for neutral-atom qubit–waveguide coupling

The figure of merit is **not** raw Q. The objective is to maximize the probability that a single photon emitted by (or absorbed by) the trapped atom goes into one chosen waveguide mode, at a position where an atom can actually be trapped. Quantitatively:

**η = [C/(C+1)] · [κ_wg/κ_tot] · η_fiber,   with C = 4g²/(κγ)**

(γ/2π ≈ 6 MHz for Rb D2). This decomposes into four requirements:

1. **Large cooperativity at the atom location, C ≫ 1.** Since g ∝ 1/√V, this means small mode volume (state of the art V ≈ 0.4–0.9 (λ/n)³, g/2π ≈ 0.3–1 GHz at realistic atom positions) and *enough* total Q. Crucially, g must be evaluated ~200–300 nm from the dielectric surface — the closest standing-wave antinode of a tweezer retro-reflected off the beam — not at the field maximum inside the dielectric. Surface (Casimir–Polder) forces set a hard floor of z₀ ≈ 100–130 nm. C ≳ 10–35 gives atom–photon entanglement fidelity ≳ 0.9 (error ~ ln C / C); the best demonstrated single-atom values are C ≈ 27–71 (Lukin/Vuletić, Bernien groups). Air-mode / sawtooth geometries push field energy into vacuum to raise g(r) at the trap site.

2. **One-sided, overcoupled output: κ_wg/κ_tot → 1.** Losses must be dominated by the intentional waveguide port, not radiation/scattering. In practice: many mirror cells on the back side, few (3–5) on the coupling side, so Q_scattering ≫ Q_waveguide. This deliberately *sacrifices* total Q (and some C) for extraction efficiency — the optimum κ_wg exceeds the critical-coupling point. The repo's directional-Q analysis exists precisely to compute this budget. Measured outcoupling fractions of 10–40% were the dominant loss in the closest predecessor work (Menon thesis); >60% is the goal.

3. **The "bad-cavity" (Purcell) regime: κ ≳ g ≫ γ, with C ≫ 1.** Strong coupling is not required; a fast κ (GHz-scale, i.e., Q ~ 10⁴–10⁵ at 384 THz) gives broadband, fast photon extraction and protocol rates ultimately limited by 1/κ. Hence high *intrinsic* (scattering-limited) Q ~ 10⁵–10⁶ from gentle confinement, then loaded Q intentionally lowered through the coupling mirror.

4. **Compatibility with a trapped atom and with fabrication.** Beam thickness/width set the tweezer reflection phase and hence the trap distance z₀ (a sawtooth function of thickness — a genuine design trade against g and Q); the taper must be Bragg-matched to the waveguide to avoid scattering; lattice-constant-only tapers are the most fabrication-robust knob (e-beam hole *positions* are far more reproducible than hole *sizes*); the design should tolerate a few-nm dimensional error, with post-fabrication thermal tuning covering the residual.

The photonic recipe that delivers (1)–(3) is the "gentle confinement" / Gaussian-envelope method: taper the mirror strength (via filling fraction or lattice constant) quadratically from the defect center so the field envelope is Gaussian, leaving minimal Fourier content inside the light cone. That is exactly the architecture the `cavity`/`defect`/`mirror`/`taper` classes implement.

# Literature summaries (`literature/`)

## Reviews

### Suleymanzade & Dimitrova, *Quantum optical interfaces for neutral atom arrays* (review, 2025)
Surveys photonic interfaces for neutral-atom tweezer arrays, motivated by distributed/modular quantum computing (target: remote Bell pairs at >10 kHz). Compares ~20 platforms — free space (η ~ 1–15%), Fabry–Perot, fiber cavities, bow-tie, nanofibers, microrings, and photonic-crystal cavities — in terms of (g, κ, γ, C). Nanophotonic cavities are the deepest "bad-cavity" realization: g/2π ~ 400–600 MHz, κ_tot ~ GHz, C = 27–71 demonstrated, atoms ~250 nm from the surface, with C > 1000 possible very close to the structure. Key formulas: C = 4g²/(κγ); η_col = [C/(C+1)]·(κ_out/κ_tot)·η_fiber. Articulates the central trade-offs: overcoupled one-sided design (κ_out/κ_tot → 1) is as important as C ≫ 1; small cavities maximize C but put atoms near surfaces (problem for Rydberg, n⁷ scaling of stray-field sensitivity); nanobeams are single-polarization, favoring time-bin encoding; ~100 cavities multiplexed on one chip is the demonstrated scaling route.

### Reiserer & Rempe, *Cavity-based quantum networks with single atoms and optical photons*, Rev. Mod. Phys. 87, 1379 (2015)
The canonical cavity-QED-network review. Derives the formalism: g = √(μ²ω/2ε₀ħV), cooperativity C = g²/(2κγ) (field-decay convention) as **the** figure of merit — cavity length cancels in C, so the real lever is mode volume; photon emission probability into the output port P = (κ_out/κ)·2C/(1+2C). Design conditions stated explicitly: C ≫ 1 *and* κ_out ≫ (γ, κ_loss). Reviews vSTIRAP photon generation (~69% efficiency), quantum memories (93% fidelity at C ≈ 1), the first elementary atom–atom network (entanglement fidelity 85%), nondestructive photon detection and atom–photon gates at C ≈ 3 via the Duan–Kimble reflection mechanism (which is robust to the exact g — works in the Purcell regime). Highlights photonic crystals as the route to GHz-scale g and large-bandwidth interfaces, and atom localization (Lamb–Dicke, Raman sideband cooling, magic traps) as critical to coupling stability.

### González-Tudela, Reiserer, García-Ripoll & García-Vidal, *Light–matter interactions in quantum nanophotonic devices*, Nat. Rev. Phys. 6, 166 (2024)
Modern review of nanophotonic light–matter interfaces across emitters (atoms, QDs, defect centers, rare earths) and photonic platforms (PhC cavities V ≈ λ³ / Q ~ 10⁴–10⁶; plasmonics V ~ 10⁻⁵λ³ / Q ~ 1–10; hybrids). Key relation for this project: waveguide β-factor β = Γ_1D/(Γ_1D+γ′) = C/(C+1), the universal collection fraction. Benchmarks for atoms: C ≈ 0.05–0.1 (nanofibers), 1–2 (PhC waveguides), ≈ 70 (tweezers near PhC cavities) — the gap attributed to the difficulty of trapping atoms near nanostructures; tweezer retro-reflection traps flagged as the enabler of C ~ O(10). SiN and silica singled out as the low-absorption materials of choice for cold-atom work. Also covers band-edge physics directly relevant to the repo's bandstructure pipeline: photon-mediated atom–atom interactions J_ij with exponential range set by detuning into the gap, super/subradiance, and chiral coupling.

## AMO experiments / proposals

### Thompson et al., *Coupling a Single Trapped Atom to a Nanoscale Optical Cavity*, Science 340, 1202 (2013) (+ Supplement)
Foundational experiment: a single ⁸⁷Rb atom in an optical tweezer, retro-reflected off the nanostructure itself, is deterministically loaded (94% fidelity) into the closest standing-wave antinode ~260 nm above a SiN photonic-crystal cavity glued to a tapered fiber tip. Cavity: SiN beam 433 × 200 nm, a = 270→290 nm quadratic taper, r = 70 nm; working mode at 779.5 nm with Q = 460, V = 0.89 λ³ → 2g/2π = 0.60(8) GHz measured, C = 0.07 (limited entirely by Q; with the already-demonstrated Q = 3×10⁵, C > 1000 projected). The supplement contains the design recipe most relevant here: fix a and thickness, sweep beam width, and solve for the hole radius that keeps the band edge fixed — a one-parameter family that tunes the trap distance z₀ (set by reflection phase, controllable via slab thickness) at constant resonance wavelength. Establishes z₀ ≈ 100 nm as the surface-force floor and polarization rules for the tweezer (parallel to beam axis to avoid vector light shifts).

### Menon, Singh, Borregaard & Bernien, *Nanophotonic quantum network node with neutral atoms and an integrated telecom interface*, New J. Phys. 22, 073033 (2020)
Design proposal closest in spirit to this repo's target: a Cs atom tweezer-trapped above a SiN nanobeam cavity (340 nm thick, 876 nm wide, Q = 2×10⁵, V = 0.7 (λ/n)³) resonant with an *excited-state telecom transition* (1360 nm), generating time-bin atom–photon entanglement without frequency conversion. Full master-equation simulation gives entanglement error ≈ ln C / C: F > 0.9 for C ≳ 10–15; at the trap region C ranges 5–260, averaging C = 35 over a realistic thermal atom volume (200×200×50 nm³) → F ≈ 0.93. Quantifies a trade-off the repo should inherit: g falls exponentially with trap distance while the polarization purity of free-space addressing beams *rises* with distance; beam thickness tunes both the trap distance (~100–500 nm) and purity. Critical coupling (κ_f = κ_l) halves the heralded success rate but not the fidelity, since photon loss is heralded. Appendix gives the Rb variant (1530 nm via 4D₃/₂↔5P₃/₂).

### Menon, Glachman, Pompili, Dibos & Bernien, *An integrated atom array–nanophotonic chip platform with background-free imaging*, Nat. Commun. 15, 6156 (2024)
Scales the interface from one device to an array: 64 Cs tweezers next to a semi-open SiN chip hosting >100 nanobeam cavities (330–340 nm thick, 1.1 µm wide, 11 µm pitch, suspended over the chip edge so MOT and tweezers have clear access). Atoms are loaded ~100 µm away and transported onto devices, trapped ~300 nm above the surface in the tweezer-reflection standing wave (verified by Stark-shift spectroscopy; projected g ~ 2π × 600 MHz at that distance). Core innovation: background-free single-shot imaging via a two-photon ladder scheme (852 + 1470 nm excitation, 895 nm detection) achieving ≥99.2% fidelity next to the structures — standard D2 imaging is drowned by device scatter. Also demonstrates free-space (lens-based) coupling into any device on the chip (20% measured, 91% simulated) and real-time rearrangement onto single or multiple cavities — the architecture this repo's cavity arrays would plug into.

### Menon, PhD thesis, *Combining Nanophotonic Cavity-Arrays with Atomic Arrays*, U. Chicago (2025)
The most complete predecessor of this repo's methodology. Documents the full design loop for the SiN telecom (1360 nm) cavities: (i) thickness fixed first (330 nm) from the *trap-distance* sawtooth, not photonics; (ii) a = λ/2n_eff starting point; mirror cell with bandgap centered on target (filling fraction 0.47), defect cell with dielectric band edge at target (0.52); (iii) quadratic, lattice-constant-only taper over 10 holes/side (hole sizes fixed for e-beam robustness), with extra back-side mirrors and 3–5 coupling-side mirrors fabricated in variants to scan κ_wg; (iv) 5 linearly tapered holes for the Bragg-matched waveguide transition, then a 1.1 µm → 200 nm waveguide taper for free-space coupling (>65% measured). Simulated intrinsic Q ≈ 2.5×10⁶; measured Q = 0.5–2.2×10⁵ with outcoupling 10–40% (identified as the main loss). System analysis: P_fiber = [4C/(4C+1)]·[κ_wg/(κ_wg+κ_sc)]·F_ce; C ≈ 35 at the trap site → F ≈ 0.93. Includes laser-based thermal tuning of individual cavities (~0.05 nm/mW, 2–3 nm range) onto atomic resonance. Everything transfers to the 780 nm Rb design after rescaling by ~780/1360.

## Photonics design papers

### Quan & Lončar, *Deterministic design of wavelength scale, ultra-high Q photonic crystal nanobeam cavities*, Opt. Express 19, 18529 (2011)
The canonical nanobeam recipe, used (adapted) by the repo's defect interpolation. Three rules: zero cavity length, constant period, and Gaussian field attenuation via *linearly increasing mirror strength* — achieved by tapering the hole filling fraction quadratically from the value that puts the dielectric band edge at the target frequency (f_start) to the value of maximum mirror strength (f_end), over N ≥ 15 cells/side. Demonstrates (Si, 1.5 µm): radiation-limited Q = 5×10⁹ at V = 0.9 (λ/n)³; waveguide-coupled Q = 1.3×10⁷ with T = 97% — the cavity couples *naturally* to the feeding waveguide, with T vs Q controlled simply by mirror count; and an **air-mode** cavity (beam-width taper at fixed holes, Q up to 1.4×10⁹) explicitly motivated by light–atom interaction in the low-index region. The resonance asymptotes to the dielectric band edge of the central cell (offset < 1% for N > 15) — the basis of designing the defect cell by band-edge placement.

### Tanaka, Asano & Noda, *Design of Photonic Crystal Nanocavity With Q-Factor of ~10⁹*, J. Lightwave Technol. 26, 1532 (2008)
The Fourier-space theory behind "gentle confinement." A cavity mode's momentum spectrum is the Bloch carrier (±π/a) convolved with the envelope spectrum; abrupt envelopes put components inside the light cone (|k| < k₀) which radiate, while a Gaussian envelope is near-optimal. Analytic condition: the band-edge detuning must grow quadratically with position so the local evanescent decay grows linearly (q = Bx). Implemented via stepped lattice constants a_n = a₀{1 − C(n+¼)²} in a Si 2D slab: Q = 5×10⁸ → 7×10⁸ after diagnosing residual leakage by inverse-transforming the light-cone components, at V = 1.3 (λ/n)³. Establishes the general principle the repo's polynomial defect function approximates: taper so the envelope is Gaussian, and diagnose Q loss in k-space.

### Bazin, Raj & Raineri, *Design of Silica Encapsulated High-Q Photonic Crystal Nanobeam Cavity*, J. Lightwave Technol. 32, 952 (2014)
Most relevant treatment of moderate index contrast and fabrication tolerance. An InP nanobeam fully embedded in SiO₂ reaches Q ≈ 10⁶ at V ~ (λ/n)³ using a **lattice-constant-only** Gaussian taper (hole radius and width fixed) — chosen because e-beam hole *positions* are accurate to fractions of a nm while hole *sizes* depend on dose and etch. Method: numerically extract the in-gap decay constant q(f, a) by FDTD, fit q = √((f − f_cut)/A), solve q(x) = Bx → analytic a(x), discretize into blocks. Encapsulation costs essentially no Q (1.67×10⁶ suspended vs 1.61×10⁶ encapsulated), and Q ≈ 10⁶ holds over ±25 nm in a₀ and ±10 nm in r, giving lithographic wavelength tuning at constant Q. Directly applicable to SiN at 780 nm (similar index-contrast regime) and to choosing fabrication-robust taper parameterizations.

### Knall et al., Supplementary Information for *Efficient Source of Shaped Single Photons Based on an Integrated Diamond Nanophotonic System*, PRL 129, 053603 (2022)
The template for the repo's one-sided cavity and taper design (cited verbatim in `cavity_tutorial.ipynb`). Diamond nanobeam at 737 nm (close to 780 nm) with SiV⁻: V = 0.67 (λ/n)³, {g, κ, γ} = 2π × {6.8 GHz, 329 GHz, 0.1 GHz}, C = 5.6, deliberately **overcoupled** — optimizing source efficiency η_s = [C/(C+1)]·[κ_w/(κ_w+κ_s)] rather than C, with optimal κ_w = √(κ_s(4g² + κ_sγ)/γ). Introduces the "quasipotential" picture: each unit cell's band-edge detuning from the resonance is a tunneling-barrier height; the output side uses a low/narrow barrier (different lattice constant, a_R = 250 vs a_L = 272 nm) for fast directional decay, the back side a high barrier. The mirror→waveguide transition taper chooses the final lattice constant to match the Bragg standing-wave period set by the waveguide n_eff, with field nodes on the holes to minimize scattering — exactly the `a_last = λ₀/2n_eff` rule implemented in this repo. Also gives the FDTD figure of merit balancing Q_scat/Q_wg directionality, asymmetry, and Q/V².
